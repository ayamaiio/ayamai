# Why we build Ayamai

> We don't build Ayamai to someday earn the freedom to make something we love. We already have that freedom. Ayamai is what we're choosing to do with it.

**Owner-approved foundational principle — 2026-10-31.** This is the canonical
statement of why Ayamai exists, carried into every agent's onboarding, execution
brief and handoff. It applies to ChatGPT Work, DeepSeek, Codex, Claude, GitHub
automation that plans or changes the project, and future agents, regardless of
where they originated. Repository access is enough to read it; conversation
history is not required.

## How we build it

> An idiot admires complexity, a genius admires simplicity.
> — Terry A. Davis, creator of TempleOS

That is the design rule the platform is held to: one door, one address, one way
in. A product is a manifest, a directory and a route; the door is a router, not a
framework. When two designs are possible, choose the one with fewer moving parts,
and say what the thing does instead of decorating it. Complexity that cannot be
explained is not sophistication — it is something to remove.

## What we choose to build for

We build for art, technology, ourselves and our friends. Making something we love
is already a worthwhile use of our freedom. Craft, useful experiments, beautiful
experiences and the pleasure of building belong in the purpose of the work.
Ayamai does not need a future financial outcome to justify that choice.

Protect creative independence, curiosity and genuine community. Favor things we
want to use and share with people we care about. Let exploration and friendship
shape the experience without turning every interaction into a growth target.

## Money sustains the work

Revenue and commercial success are welcome when they help sustain the project,
its people and its independence. This is no blanket hostility to business,
profit, charging for value or reaching more people. Money should sustain the
product, not dictate what it becomes. Commercial possibilities remain subject to
the owner's existing decision and implementation process.

Guard against incentive drift: engagement, retention, conversion and profit are
possible signals, not the purpose of Ayamai. Do not quietly substitute maximizing
time spent, compulsive use, monetization or scale for the experience we chose to
build. Explain the tradeoff when a proposal would sacrifice privacy, craft,
curiosity, autonomy or genuine relationships for those metrics.

## How every agent carries this forward

- On opening or cloning the repository, read this document alongside root
  [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) before planning or implementing work.
- Use this principle in product, design and technical tradeoffs. Explain material
  conflicts and follow the existing owner-authority and approval rules; do not
  invent a roadmap, reject a commercial idea automatically or expand task scope.
- Include this canonical reference in agent entrypoints, execution briefs and
  handoffs. When the receiving agent cannot access repository links, copy the
  approved quote and the relevant guidance into its self-contained brief.
- When adding a new agent integration or instruction file, point it here and to
  root AGENTS.md so the principle survives changes of tools, models and hosts.

This principle guides judgment. It grants no additional access, implementation,
merge or deployment authority and creates no executable authentication or release
gate. Existing security, verification and operational instructions still apply.
