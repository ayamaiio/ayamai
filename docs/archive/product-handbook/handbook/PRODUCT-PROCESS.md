# Product Process

This process keeps exploration productive without accidentally turning every thought into scope.

## Idea lifecycle

| Status | Meaning | May create Linear work? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw idea | A thought worth remembering | No |
| Exploring | We are discussing the concept and alternatives | No |
| Candidate | It appears promising and may deserve prioritization | Not yet |
| Approved direction | The owner explicitly accepts it as product direction | Not automatically |
| Committed | The owner authorizes planning and execution | Yes |
| Implemented | Built and reviewed against the intended outcome | Follow-up work only if needed |
| Parked | Deferred for later consideration | No active work |
| Rejected | Explicitly decided against | No |

An idea can move backward, be split, or be abandoned. Promotion requires explicit owner approval.

## Open Product Questions

An **Open Product Question** is a meaningful product question that is intentionally unanswered. Use this when the owner has no current preference, needs more context, or simply does not want to decide yet.

This is different from a product idea: an idea describes a possibility; an open question records uncertainty that may affect a future product decision.

For each open question, capture:
- The question in neutral language
- Relevant context or why it matters
- Current status: unanswered, intentionally deferred, or ready to revisit
- Any known options or tradeoffs, without implying a recommendation is approved
- A revisit trigger, such as before finalizing the relevant experience, approving a feature brief, or committing dependent implementation

Guidelines:
- “I don't have an opinion yet” is a valid outcome. Do not force a decision.
- Do not interpret silence, casual agreement, or exploration as approval.
- Keep open questions visible in the relevant product exploration document and/or a consolidated open-questions list.
- Revisit them at the agreed trigger, not on every conversation turn.
- An open question is not a blocker unless the owner or delivery plan explicitly makes it one.
- Resolve it only through an explicit owner decision; otherwise it can remain open, be reframed, or be retired.

Use **Product Reference** for durable examples, precedents, inspiration, or source material—not as the label for unresolved decisions.

## Typical path

1. **Notice:** Capture the user need, frustration, opportunity, or idea.
2. **Explore:** Clarify the intended experience. Consider alternatives, edge cases, and tradeoffs.
3. **Frame:** Describe the problem, target context, desired outcome, and what success would feel like.
4. **Decide:** The owner chooses whether to reject, park, continue exploring, approve direction, or commit.
5. **Specify:** For committed work, write a feature brief with scope, non-goals, acceptance criteria, dependencies, and open questions.
6. **Design:** Use Figma when visual exploration, interaction design, or a prototype will reduce uncertainty.
7. **Plan:** Add approved, actionable work to Linear.
8. **Handoff:** Give Replit or another implementation agent a self-contained brief, constraints, relevant links, and verification expectations.
9. **Review:** Compare the implementation with the intended experience. Record gaps and decisions.

Not every idea needs every step. Do not manufacture process for small changes.

## What makes a useful feature brief?

- Problem or opportunity
- Intended user and context
- Desired experience/outcome
- Why it matters
- Scope
- Explicit non-goals
- Functional and UX expectations
- Acceptance criteria
- Privacy, safety, and accessibility considerations where relevant
- Dependencies and open questions
- Current status and owner approval

## Linear hygiene

Only create issues for work the owner has committed to doing or explicitly asked to track. Keep exploratory ideas in the handbook or conversation, not the active execution backlog.

Use projects for meaningful initiatives, not every idea. Keep the workflow lightweight for a solo product owner.

## Decision records

Record durable decisions when they materially constrain product direction, experience, policy, or architecture. Include:
- Context
- Decision
- Alternatives considered
- Consequences and tradeoffs
- Date/status
- Whether the decision supersedes an earlier one

A recommendation is not a decision record until accepted by the owner.
